Understand shift rotas and working hours in Danish

A rota tells you when you need to work. You may also need to ask about a change, a break or extra hours. Learn to find the key details and use short questions when something is unclear.

JobVocabulary · 21.11.2026

Start practising

Find the right day and the current rota

Start with the date, weekday and time. Read both the start and finish, and check which week the rota covers. A shift may take place in the evening or at night, so connect each time with the correct date. Ask Hvor kan jeg se vagtplanen? if you do not know where to find the current rota.

A change might arrive in an app, on paper or in a message. Ask which version applies if two messages disagree. Repeat the specific day and time in your reply so your colleague can correct a misunderstanding.

Distinguish a break from a day off

En pause is a break during the working day. En fridag is a day when you are not working. Hvornår holder vi pause? asks when the break is. Practise these words with a clock time, as well as i dag, i morgen and i weekenden: today, tomorrow and at the weekend.

If you cannot work at a particular time, say so clearly: Jeg kan ikke arbejde i weekenden. Add the date or weekend you mean. Knowing how to ask about working hours does not tell you your employment terms; clarify specific arrangements and payment questions with your workplace.

Ask about changes and confirm the arrangement

Kan jeg bytte vagt med dig? asks a colleague about swapping shifts. It does not mean the rota has already changed. Clarify which two shifts you mean and who needs to approve or record the change at your workplace. Then check that the correct rota has been updated.

Practise a short conversation: ask about the rota, suggest a specific change and repeat what you have agreed. You can also practise Jeg vil gerne have flere timer and Jeg vil gerne arbejde færre timer: asking for more or fewer working hours. Save words such as vagtplan, pause and weekendvagt in a personal list if you meet them often.
