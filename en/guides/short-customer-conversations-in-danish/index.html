Short customer conversations in Danish

A customer conversation can be short. What matters is understanding what the customer needs and making the next step clear. Start with a few phrases for a shop, then adapt your practice to other jobs involving customer contact.

JobVocabulary · 21.11.2026

Start practising

Start with one question

Say Velkommen. and ask Kan jeg hjælpe dig? Give the customer time to answer. Hvad leder du efter? helps you find out which product or department they mean. Listen for the product name and any size, colour or quantity.

Practise these questions as the start of a short exchange. Change the product name and give a brief answer. Hotels, restaurants and offices involve different situations, but greeting someone and finding out what they need are useful in each setting.

Ask to hear it again

Use Kan du sige det igen? if you do not understand the reply. You do not need to guess the whole message. Repeat the word, number or time you did hear and ask the customer to confirm it. This is particularly useful when they mention several products or quantities.

Understanding the question and knowing the answer are separate steps. You may understand what a customer wants without yet knowing whether it is in stock. Practise both the customer’s request and your own short explanation of what you are checking.

Explain what happens next

Et øjeblik, tak. tells the customer there will be a short wait. Jeg spørger lige min kollega. explains how you will get help. Use Kom med, så viser jeg dig det. when you can actually show the customer the right place.

Practise a short sequence: greet the customer, ask what they need, request a repetition and explain the next step. Check before promising that something is available or possible. Save the phrases you often need in a personal list and practise them with both recognition and writing tasks.

Danish words and phrases in practice

DanishEnglish
Velkommen.Welcome.
Kan jeg hjælpe dig?Can I help you?
Hvad leder du efter?What are you looking for?

These are language practice examples. Adapt the wording to your own situation.
