Numbers, time and dates in Danish

Numbers and time expressions appear in work schedules, addresses, appointments and transport messages. Practise recognising them on their own and inside short sentences.

JobVocabulary · 20.11.2026

Start practising

Separate clock times from duration

Klokken introduces a time of day. Timer and minutter describe a duration. Compare jeg møder klokken otte, meaning I start at eight, with jeg arbejder i otte timer, meaning I work for eight hours.

Danish half-hour expressions point towards the following hour. Halv otte means 7:30, not 8:30. Kvart over otte is 8:15, and kvart i otte is 7:45. Begin with whole hours and add these expressions gradually.

Put weekdays into sentences

Learn Monday to Sunday alongside i dag, i morgen and i går: today, tomorrow and yesterday. Then combine a day with an action, such as jeg arbejder på fredag, meaning I work on Friday.

Say the day and month together when practising a date. Learn month names in the situations where you need them, rather than only reciting January to December.

Practise with everyday situations

Read a time from a sample shift schedule and say it aloud. Write a short message explaining when you will arrive. Use fictional appointments if you do not want personal details in your word lists.

Everyday essentials groups numbers, time and calendar vocabulary into topics. Select several topics together or focus on the ones you find most difficult.
