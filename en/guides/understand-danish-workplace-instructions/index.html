Understand Danish instructions at work

A short instruction often contains an action, an object and a place. Recognising these parts helps you make sense of new messages without knowing every possible sentence in advance.

JobVocabulary · 20.11.2026

Start practising

Recognise the action

Words such as hent, læg, tag, tør and vent tell you what to do: fetch, put, take, wipe and wait. Practise them with objects from your job. Hent vognen and hent kassen use the same action with different objects.

A colleague may use a command rather than the dictionary form. You might learn at hente in a vocabulary list and hear hent at work. Practising both words and phrases helps you recognise the connection.

Listen for location and order

På, under, ved siden af and bagved describe where something belongs: on, under, beside and behind. Først, derefter and til sidst mean first, then and finally.

Try moving an object or pointing to a place while you say the instruction. Start with an action you know, then change the object or location. This makes the vocabulary easier to connect to an actual task.

Ask a short clarifying question

Repeat the part you understood and ask about the missing detail. You can ask which box, which shelf or which room your colleague means. These questions are useful vocabulary in their own right.
