Write emails and messages in Danish at work

A message does not have to be long to be useful. When writing in Danish to a colleague or customer, a clear purpose, specific details and a next step help. Start with small sentences you can combine.

JobVocabulary · 21.11.2026

Start practising

Start with what the reader needs to know

Say why you are contacting the person first. Tak for din besked means thank you for your message, while Jeg sender dig oplysningerne explains that you will send the details. Then add the subject, name or order number the reader needs to understand your message.

Use short sentences while you learn. A specific question is often easier to answer than several questions in one line. For example, write Kan du kontrollere oplysningerne? and explain which details you mean. Match your greeting and tone to the way people write at your workplace.

Make files, dates and arrangements clear

Et bilag is a file attached to a message. Dokumentet er vedhæftet is a simple way to say the document is attached. Also check that you have attached the right file and that its name makes the subject clear.

If something is missing or will not open, ask Kan du sende filen igen? Read dates, times and numbers once more before sending. I morgen means tomorrow, which depends on when the message is read. Use a specific date if the timing could otherwise be misunderstood.

Finish with the next step and practise your own message

Explain whether you are waiting for a reply, forwarding something or getting back to the person. Jeg vender tilbage til dig i morgen is a specific promise to reply tomorrow, so use it only when you can keep that arrangement. Har du modtaget min e-mail? asks the reader to confirm receipt.

Practise a message in three short sentences: why you are writing, what the reader needs to know and what happens next. Save unfamiliar words such as vedhæftet and oplysninger in your own list. The practice below focuses on emails and follow-up without making you complete the rest of the office course first.
