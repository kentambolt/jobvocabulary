Danish words and phrases for a job interview

A job interview may involve introducing yourself, describing previous tasks and explaining when you can work. Prepare short answers that are true for you instead of memorising a long speech.

JobVocabulary · 20.11.2026

Start practising

Describe your experience

Start with your name and the kind of job you are applying for. Practise naming tasks you have done, equipment you have used and routines you know. Choose vocabulary from the course closest to the role.

Jeg har arbejdet med rengøring means I have worked in cleaning. Use that kind of sentence as a starting point, then add a specific example from your own experience. Replace the examples with details that apply to you.

Discuss working hours and tasks

Vagt, deltid, fuldtid, weekend and mødetid refer to shifts, part-time work, full-time work, weekends and starting times. Practise understanding questions about when you can start and when you are available.

Prepare questions of your own about the job. Hvilke opgaver får jeg? asks what tasks you will have. Hvordan bliver jeg oplært? asks how you will be trained.

Practise asking for clarification

You may need to ask someone to repeat a question or explain that you are learning Danish. Saying these phrases aloud before the interview can help you find them when you need them.

Create a personal list of the expressions you want to practise. Include professional vocabulary and the short connecting words you use when describing what you have done.

Danish words and phrases in practice

DanishEnglish
Jeg søger arbejde som køkkenmedhjælper.I am looking for work as a kitchen assistant.
Jeg har erfaring med rengøring.I have experience in cleaning.
Jeg kan arbejde om aftenen.I can work in the evenings.
Hvordan bliver jeg oplært?How will I be trained?
Jeg er ved at lære dansk.I am learning Danish.

These are language practice examples. Adapt the wording to your own situation.
