Danish for your first job

Starting a job in Denmark means meeting new names, places and routines. Begin with the Danish you can use during a real shift: understanding a short instruction, finding something and asking a colleague for help.

JobVocabulary · 20.11.2026

Start practising

Choose vocabulary from your working day

Start with the course closest to your job. Cleaning vocabulary introduces rooms, tools and materials. Warehouse work brings words about goods, locations and deliveries. In a kitchen, ingredients, equipment and short instructions are useful starting points.

Choose one topic rather than trying to learn every professional word at once. Imagine where you would hear each word and what you would need to do or say in that situation.

Learn questions as well as nouns

Knowing the name of a tool is useful, but so is being able to ask where it is. Learn short questions about place, time and order alongside the objects you work with.

Practise replacing one word in a sentence. Hvor er kosten? means Where is the broom? You can replace kosten with the name of another object. This turns a sentence you know into something you can use repeatedly.

Build a list from your own workplace

Begin with the essentials in your chosen course, or take the level test if you already know some Danish. Use the heart to save words you meet, and create a personal list for tools, shift instructions or unfamiliar expressions.

You can alternate recognising words with spelling them. Select one task type or several before you start, and keep practising the vocabulary that is relevant to your work.
