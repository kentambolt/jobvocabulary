Phone calls in Danish at work

On the phone, you cannot point to an object or rely on facial expressions. A simple structure helps: say who is speaking, confirm the important details and agree what happens next. Practise each part separately before putting a whole conversation together.

JobVocabulary · 21.11.2026

Start practising

Start with your name and the reason for the call

When answering, you can give your workplace or role followed by your name. Goddag, du taler med receptionen means Hello, you are speaking to reception. Adapt the greeting to the way your workplace handles calls.

When making a call, state the reason briefly. Jeg ringer angående vores aftale means I am calling about our appointment. Ask who you should speak to if you are unsure. Practise with a colleague and take turns calling and answering.

Confirm names and numbers

Ask for a name to be spelt or a number to be repeated when you are unsure. Write it down and read it back. Checking one detail is more useful than guessing from the rest of the conversation.

Begin with a name and a phone number, then add a weekday and time. Så vi ses i morgen klokken ni? checks that you are meeting tomorrow at nine. You can also ask the caller to speak more slowly when the pace is difficult to follow.

Finish with a clear next step

You do not need to resolve every question during the call. You can transfer it, take a message or arrange another call. Say what you will do: Jeg giver beskeden videre means I will pass on the message. Only promise a response time you can commit to.

The exercise below starts directly with phone calls and appointments from Office and customer service. Save phrases you use in your own role in a personal list. Practise a short conversation from both sides, then repeat the agreement and say goodbye.
